RESEARCH ARTICLE

Can a Computer Game Improve Emotion Recognition and Understanding in Children with Hearing Impairments?

A research-informed summary of the EMOFIT study, exploring how an accessible computer game may support facial emotion recognition and emotional understanding among children with hearing impairments.

Published September 2025 Journal of Elementary Education

Why this matters

Understanding emotions is a key part of communication

Recognizing and understanding facial emotions are important skills for social communication. Children with hearing impairments may experience difficulties interpreting emotional and social cues, particularly when emotional meaning depends on language, contextual information, or subtle facial expressions.

Digital learning environments may provide an opportunity to support these skills through structured visual information, interaction, immediate feedback, and gradual increases in task difficulty.

The study

What did we investigate?

The study examined the effectiveness of EMOFIT (Emotion-Focused Interactive Training), a puzzle-based computer game designed to support facial emotion recognition and understanding in children with hearing impairments.

Thirty children between the ages of 6 and 10 years participated in the study. Participants were assigned to an experimental group using EMOFIT and a control group receiving regular activities.

Study design

Quasi-experimental pre-test/post-test design with experimental and control groups.

The intervention

What is EMOFIT?

EMOFIT is a puzzle-based educational computer game designed around a progression from basic emotion recognition to more contextualized emotional understanding.

The game included eight stages addressing four progressively complex skills:

01 · Differentiation

Identifying and distinguishing basic facial emotions such as happiness, sadness, anger, fear, surprise, and disgust.

02 · Naming

Identifying and verbally labelling the emotion represented by a facial expression.

03 · Correspondence

Connecting an emotional expression with an emotionally meaningful situation.

04 · Adaptation

Inferring how a person might feel from a situation when the emotional expression is not explicitly provided.

How the game worked

From recognizing faces to understanding situations

EMOFIT used visual cues, puzzle pieces, human characters, culturally relevant situations, progressive challenges, and immediate feedback. Children selected an emotion and then constructed the corresponding facial expression using puzzle pieces.

As the stages progressed, the task moved beyond simply identifying an expression. Children were gradually asked to connect emotions with situations and eventually infer emotions from contextual scenarios.

Intervention duration

Twelve 30-minute sessions conducted across five weeks.

Key findings

What did we find?

Children who participated in the EMOFIT intervention demonstrated significantly greater improvements than the control group in both facial emotion recognition and emotion understanding.

What does this mean?

Beyond recognizing an emotion

One of the most interesting findings was that improvement was not limited to recognizing facial expressions. The intervention had an even stronger effect on emotion understanding, particularly the ability to infer emotions from contextual situations.

This distinction is important. Recognizing that a face looks "happy" is different from understanding why someone might feel happy in a particular situation.

The findings suggest that educational games may be particularly valuable when they move learners from isolated visual recognition toward contextualized interpretation.

Design implications

What can we learn about inclusive educational technology?

The study highlights several design principles that may be useful when developing digital learning environments for children with hearing impairments:

  • Use clear and meaningful visual cues.
  • Keep interfaces simple and free from unnecessary visual distractions.
  • Introduce challenges progressively.
  • Provide immediate and understandable feedback.
  • Connect learning tasks to realistic and culturally relevant situations.
  • Move gradually from simple recognition toward contextual understanding.

Practical implications

Where could this approach be useful?

The findings indicate potential applications for educational technology in settings such as special education classrooms and speech-language therapy centres.

Importantly, EMOFIT should not be viewed as a replacement for professional educational or therapeutic support. Rather, game-based tools may function as an additional structured learning environment for practicing emotional and social skills.

Limitations

What should we be cautious about?

The study involved a relatively small sample of 30 children from one cultural context. Differences among participants, including degree of hearing loss, time since diagnosis, and previous therapy experience, may also influence the findings.

In addition, the study examined outcomes immediately following the intervention. Further research is needed to determine whether these improvements are maintained over time and whether they transfer to children's everyday social interactions.

Future studies should therefore examine larger and more diverse samples and include longer-term follow-up assessments.

Takeaway

A small game can address a meaningful educational need

The EMOFIT study suggests that thoughtfully designed educational games can support both facial emotion recognition and deeper emotional understanding in children with hearing impairments.

The strongest improvement was observed in adaptation — the ability to infer emotions from contextual situations. This finding points to an important principle for inclusive educational technology: effective digital learning should not only present information visually, but should help learners connect that information to meaningful social contexts.
